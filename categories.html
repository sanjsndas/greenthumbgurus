In-Depth Category Analysis

1. Soil Health & Remediation

Soil is the living matrix that sustains all terrestrial life. Unfortunately, most suburban and urban soils have been severely degraded by construction activities, compaction, chemical fertilizers, and erosion. Our soil health category is the cornerstone of every revival project. We begin with a comprehensive soil analysis that measures pH, nutrient levels, organic matter content, and microbial activity. This data informs a tailored remediation plan that may include:

  • Mechanical Aeration: Using core aerators to relieve compaction and allow oxygen, water, and nutrients to penetrate the root zone.
  • Organic Amendments: Application of mature compost, worm castings, and aged manures to build soil structure and fertility.
  • Biochar Incorporation: Adding this stable form of charcoal to enhance water and nutrient retention while providing habitat for beneficial fungi.
  • Compost Tea Inoculation: Spraying a liquid concentrate of beneficial microbes to jumpstart the soil food web.
  • Mycorrhizal Inoculants: Introducing symbiotic fungi that form networks with plant roots, dramatically improving nutrient and water uptake.

The results of our soil remediation are transformative: plants become more vigorous, disease resistance increases, and water requirements decrease significantly. Healthy soil also acts as a carbon sink, helping to mitigate climate change. This category is essential for any garden that has been neglected or subjected to conventional landscaping practices.

2. Native Planting & Ecological Design

Once the soil is prepared, we turn our attention to the living palette. Our native planting category is rooted in the belief that gardens should reflect and support the unique ecology of their region. We design plant communities that mimic natural ecosystems, creating layered habitats that provide food, shelter, and breeding grounds for local wildlife. Key elements include:

  • Plant Selection: We curate a list of native trees, shrubs, perennials, and grasses that are adapted to your specific climate, soil type, and sun exposure.
  • Successional Planting: We design for year-round interest, ensuring that something is always blooming or providing structure throughout the seasons.
  • Pollinator Corridors: We prioritize plants that support bees, butterflies, hummingbirds, and other essential pollinators.
  • Meadow and Prairie Restoration: For larger properties, we convert lawns into diverse meadows that require less mowing and provide critical habitat.

Native plants are inherently resilient, requiring less water, fewer inputs, and no synthetic fertilizers once established. They form the backbone of a sustainable garden that thrives in harmony with its surroundings. Our designs are both beautiful and functional, creating spaces that invite you to connect with the natural world.

3. Edible Landscapes & Food Forests

Why choose between an ornamental garden and a vegetable patch? Our edible landscaping category merges the two, creating productive landscapes that are as beautiful as they are bountiful. We design multi-layered food forests that mimic the structure of a natural woodland, maximizing productivity in a small footprint. This approach includes:

  • Canopy Layer: Fruit and nut trees (apples, pears, walnuts, pecans) that provide shade and high yields.
  • Understory Layer: Smaller fruit trees (dwarf cherries, pawpaws, persimmons) and berry shrubs (blueberries, currants, gooseberries).
  • Herbaceous Layer: Perennial vegetables (asparagus, rhubarb, artichokes) and culinary herbs (rosemary, thyme, sage) that add flavor and texture.
  • Groundcover Layer: Creeping plants (strawberries, creeping thyme) that suppress weeds and retain moisture.
  • Root Layer: Edible roots (sunchokes, garlic, potatoes) that harvest beneath the surface.

These systems are designed to be low-maintenance once established, with the plants supporting each other through nutrient cycling and natural pest suppression. They provide a continuous supply of fresh, organic produce for your table, connecting you to the rhythms of the seasons and the land.

Specialized Services for Complex Challenges

4. Invasive Species Management

Invasive species are one of the greatest threats to biodiversity and garden health. They outcompete native plants, alter soil chemistry, and degrade wildlife habitat. Our invasive species management category is a specialized service designed to safely and effectively remove aggressive invaders from your property. We employ a variety of techniques, including:

  • Manual Removal: Hand-pulling, digging, and cutting for small infestations or in sensitive areas where herbicides are not appropriate.
  • Sheet Mulching: Smothering invasive plants with layers of cardboard and wood chips to block light and deplete root reserves.
  • Targeted Herbicide Application: When manual methods are insufficient, we use low-impact herbicides applied by trained professionals to minimize collateral damage.
  • Follow-Up Monitoring: Invasive species often require multiple years of management to fully eradicate. We provide ongoing monitoring and maintenance to ensure they do not return.

Our goal is not just to remove invasive species but to restore the ecological balance of your garden, creating space for native plants to re-establish and thrive. We take a long-term view, recognizing that recovery is a process that requires patience and persistence.

5. Waterwise & Stormwater Solutions

Water is the lifeblood of any garden, but it is also a finite and increasingly precious resource. Our waterwise category focuses on designing and installing systems that minimize water use while maximizing infiltration and reducing runoff. Key offerings include:

  • Rain Gardens: Shallow depressions planted with water-tolerant natives that capture and filter runoff from roofs and driveways.
  • Swales and Berms: Contoured earthworks that slow and spread water across the landscape, allowing it to soak into the soil.
  • Permeable Hardscaping: Driveways, patios, and pathways constructed from porous materials that allow water to infiltrate rather than run off.
  • Drip Irrigation: Highly efficient irrigation systems that deliver water directly to plant roots, minimizing evaporation and waste.
  • Graywater Systems: Reusing water from sinks, showers, and washing machines to irrigate ornamental plants and fruit trees.

These solutions not only conserve water but also reduce the burden on municipal stormwater systems, preventing pollution of local waterways. They transform your garden into a sponge that absorbs and filters rainfall, contributing to a healthier local ecosystem.

6. Hardscaping & Living Structures

Hardscaping provides the bones of your garden—the structures that define spaces, create flow, and offer functional surfaces for seating, dining, and circulation. Our hardscaping category emphasizes the use of natural, sustainable materials that blend seamlessly with the surrounding landscape. We offer:

  • Natural Stone Pathways: Flagstone, slate, or cobblestone paths that meander through the garden, inviting exploration.
  • Retaining Walls: Dry-stack or mortared stone walls that manage slopes, create terraced planting areas, and add visual interest.
  • Raised Beds: Constructed from rot-resistant wood or stone, raised beds improve drainage, extend the growing season, and make gardening more accessible.
  • Pergolas and Arbors: Timber structures that provide shade, support climbing vines, and define outdoor rooms.
  • Living Fences and Screens: Dense hedges of native shrubs or espaliered fruit trees that provide privacy, wind protection, and wildlife habitat.

Our designs balance aesthetic appeal with ecological function, ensuring that every hardscaping element contributes to the overall health and beauty of your garden.

7. Arboriculture & Tree Care

Mature trees are among the most valuable assets in any landscape. They provide shade, filter air, support wildlife, and increase property value. Our arboriculture category is dedicated to the health and safety of your trees. Services include:

  • Structural Pruning: Selective removal of branches to improve form, eliminate hazards, and promote healthy growth.
  • Crown Reduction and Thinning: Reducing the weight and density of a tree's canopy to prevent storm damage and improve light penetration.
  • Root Zone Management: Aeration, mulching, and mycorrhizal inoculation to support root health and vitality.
  • Hazard Assessment: Detailed inspections to identify structural weaknesses, disease, or pest infestations that could pose a risk.
  • Tree Planting and Establishment: Expert planting of new trees with proper staking, watering, and aftercare to ensure successful establishment.

We treat each tree as an individual, developing a care plan that addresses its specific needs and conditions. Proper tree care not only enhances the beauty of your garden but also ensures the safety and longevity of these magnificent living structures.
